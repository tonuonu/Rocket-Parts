## 1. The project

Apogee Peregrine rocket, 100 mm body tube (measured OD 101.5, ID 99.1).
The nosecone is 3D-printed in slices, generated by OpenSCAD:

- Source: `PeregrineCamNose.scad` (repo root), built on the library
  `NoseCone.scad`. Run OpenSCAD with `OPENSCADPATH=<repo>`.
- Printable meshes and the print/assembly notes:
  `STL Files/PeregrineCamNose/` (`README.md` there).
- Geometry checks: `python3 tools/verify_camnose.py [part numbers]`
  (renders with `/Applications/OpenSCAD-dev.app`, checks dims, holes, fits).
- Export one part:
  `OPENSCADPATH=$PWD /Applications/OpenSCAD-dev.app/Contents/MacOS/OpenSCAD --render --export-format binstl -o out.stl -D Render_Part=N PeregrineCamNose.scad`
- `Render_Part` selects the part: 0 test ring, 1 shoulder, 2 bottom slice,
  3 middle slice, 4 top slice, 5 adapter. Keep it committed as `0`.

Stack, bottom to top (Z in mm, Z=0 at the nosecone base):

| Part | Z range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Shoulder | below 0, 100 mm long | Slip fit in the body tube (removed every flight). Has a 4 mm bulkhead with strap/rope slots — this is the existing "bottom wall". Spigot glued into the bottom slice. |
| 2 Bottom slice | 0 – 147.15 | Ogive shell, wall 2.2 |
| 3 Middle slice | 147.15 – 294.29 | Joints between slices are 7 mm glued flange laps (epoxy today) |

Bolts to the adapter with 3 × M3 on a Ø37.96 circle at 52.2°, −52.2°, 180° (deliberately not 120° — keys the camera's clocking). |

## 5. Schurter 0033.4513 (datasheet in `docs/datasheets/`)

Source: Schurter SWA1 (Frontpl) datasheet, dated 01.09.2025,
https://www.schurter.com/en/datasheet/typ_SWA1__Frontpl_.pdf
(also `docs/datasheets/Schurter_SWA1_Frontpl_0033.4513.pdf`).

- 2-position series/parallel voltage selector, markings LO / HI.
  **Actuated by screwdriver/coin slot 5.5 × 1.6 mm** — no lever. Rotation
  angle not given on the drawing; check on a real part.
- Front bezel Ø15.2, stands 2.3 mm proud of the panel. Body depth behind
  panel ≈18.5 mm, ≈23.5 mm to terminal tips (derived: drawing gives 20.8 /
  25.8 from the front face).
- **Panel cutout: Ø12.7 (+0/−0.1) round with an R1.2 anti-rotation key
  notch; notch top to opposite hole edge 13.6 ±0.1.** Model the notch.
- Panel thickness max 8.5 mm (no minimum given). Shell wall here is 2.2 —
  a local boss/pad will likely be needed.
- **Retained by a nut** on an M12.7×1.5 thread: plastic nut 0098.0043,
  14 mm across flats (datasheet in `docs/datasheets/`). Nut may be a
  separate order. Leave room behind the panel for the hex.
- 4 solder tabs 2.2 × 1.4 mm. Contacts: LO = 1–2 and 3–4 closed;
  HI = 2–3 closed. **On/off: use 2+3 (on at HI)**, or 1+2 / 3+4 (on at LO).
- Rated 6.3 A / 250 VAC. **No DC rating. Life 300 cycles (no load).**
  The owner chose this part; flag the DC-rating/cycle-life facts, don't
  replace it.

## 6. Draft layout proposed to the owner (guess — not yet confirmed)

- Upper floor: DC-DC lying flat in an epoxy tray just below the adapter
  spigot.
- Lower floor: both 18650 upright side by side in an epoxy tray, so
  acceleration loads the cells end-on.
- Blast wall below the battery floor: solid except one small sealed wire
  pass-through; it also carries the battery load. The shoulder bulkhead
  stays as is.
- Switch and the M3 countersunk charging contacts in the shell wall of the
  bay, with internal bosses / inserts.
- Detachable joints: alignment pins + M3 screws into heat-set inserts at
  the slice laps instead of epoxy; the joint opened most often is the one
  that gives access to the bay (likely adapter ↔ top slice).

## 7. Open questions (ask the owner, one at a time)

1. **Which joints must open regularly** — only the one to reach the bay,
   or every slice joint? (Was the pending question when the session ended.)
2. Cells in **series (2S) or parallel (1S2P)**? Decides charging contact
   count (2 vs 3 with a balance tap) and the DC-DC input range.
3. Confirm upright cells and the floor order (DC-DC above, cells below).
4. Is the pyro charge below the shoulder bulkhead (in the body tube)?
   Sets where the blast wall is and how much it must take.
5. Where should the switch and the charge contacts face (clocking relative
   to the camera / rail buttons)?
6. Where do the wires go: through the adapter spigot to the camera only, or
   also down to a flight computer / pyro board?

## 10. Continuation: owner decisions and implemented prototype

The owner prioritised assembly and charging; making every joint detachable
was optional. Cells will be in series, but the owner will solder the existing
tabs and decide the wiring. After viewing the bay, the owner suggested
combining the adapter with the shell if servicing still worked. **The default
upper slice now includes the camera flange in one print** and opens at the
middle-to-top joint with three side screws. This CAD change is not permission
to merge a GitHub PR.

`PeregrineCamBay.scad`, included by the camera source, implements:

- A 4 mm carrier floor at Z=270, on a corbel-supported middle-slice seat.
  Three scallops pass the joint bosses, retaining at least 3 mm nominal
  seat/seal land below them.
- Upright cells, nominal wrapped Ø18.5 × 65 body, with tab reliefs and
  insulated annular lower supports / removable upper keeper.
- Converter tray floor Z=347–350, component envelope Z=350–363, two flush
  attachment screws, cable-tie tunnels and a shallow adhesive bath.
- Integral camera flange Z=383.23–389.23, original insert pattern and
  Ø29.56 harness bore. No adapter spigot or glue joint in the default top;
  part 5 is legacy only.
- Schurter keyed cutout and flat nut-bearing pad, plus three unassigned
  mechanical M3 charging-screw seats; owner supplies the electrical work.

`STL Files/PeregrineCamNose/README.md` is the current assembly guide. Parts
3/4 replace the old middle/top; 6/7 are new. The top prints flange-down.
Shoulder and bottom are unchanged. Actual cell/tab fit, printed tolerances,
sealing, thermal and flight loads remain unverified. The barrier is not
pressure-rated. No hardware operation or GitHub merge is part of this work.
